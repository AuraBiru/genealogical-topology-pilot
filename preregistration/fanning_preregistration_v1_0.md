# **Pre-registered Predictions for Large-scale Directional Anisotropy at z = 3–10**

_Author: Aura Biru (Independent Researcher)_

_Registration date: 17 August 2026_

## **Background**

Analysis of 6 independent directional cosmological anomalies spanning _z = 0.05_ to _z = 1100_, each verified against its primary measurement paper, reveals a fanning pattern: angular deviation from a preferred sky direction increases monotonically with redshift. From the pre-specified Great Attractor reference direction, the correlation is Spearman _ρ = 0.986_, exact one-sided permutation _p = 0.0028_ (n = 6). Adding a seventh, currently contested measurement (the GRB Hubble-constant dipole, see Dataset note [a]) gives _ρ = 0.955_, exact _p = 0.0020_ (n = 7). The pattern is present with or without the contested point.

A full-sky scan for the reference direction that maximises the correlation finds an optimum at (l = 324°, b = −9°), 24° from the Great Attractor, with the same maximal ρ = 0.986; the scan does not survive look-elsewhere correction (Monte Carlo _p = 0.27_). The pre-specified Great Attractor direction is therefore already statistically optimal, and all predictions in this registration are issued from that single, pre-specified reference.

Three functional forms fit the observed fanning pattern. The sigmoid is the best fit by AIC on the core dataset (ΔAIC ≈ 9 over logarithmic) and implies the fanning saturates near 95° by z ~ 1. The logarithmic form implies continued fanning; extrapolated to 180° of angular deviation it yields a pre-CMB "trunk" of approximately 29 branch-depth e-folds, with a wide propagated uncertainty band (16th–84th percentile: 15 to 65 e-folds) that overlaps, without independently pinpointing, the Planck 2018 inflationary range of N = 50–60. The linear form implies accelerating divergence. AIC preference at n = 6 is weak evidence; a single measurement in the data gap will discriminate the families directly.

The critical data gap lies between z ≈ 2 (the contested GRB dipole) and z = 1100 (CMB anomalies). A single directional measurement at z = 3–10 with sufficient angular precision would determine whether the fanning saturates or continues, and whether the trunk extrapolation is physically meaningful.

## **Theoretical motivation**

These predictions arise from a broader ontological framework (Biru et al. 2026a, 2026b) which argues deductively that reality has a branching descriptive structure. Observable cosmology is predicted to exhibit directional coherence across heterogeneous physical phenomena, and the angular deviation from a common ancestral direction should increase with redshift: a "fanning" signature of the underlying tree-like topology. The branching topology is a structural, non-causal feature of the underlying ontology; no dynamical mechanism in the standard physical sense is proposed. The predictions are purely geometric and follow from the assumption that angular divergence with redshift traces genealogical depth. The present registration tests this prediction independently of the framework's acceptance.

## **Risk statement**

We acknowledge that our predictions are high-risk, theory-driven, and derive from an unconventional philosophical foundation. The fanning pattern was discovered during exploratory analysis and is being pre-registered for prospective validation. The statistical basis rests on 6 verified data points; the correlation is significant (exact permutation p = 0.0028) but the sample is small, the functional forms are fitted with 2–3 free parameters each, and the sole intermediate-redshift point is contested in the literature. We register these predictions as an exploratory test, not as a mature empirical forecast.

## **Reference direction**

**Great Attractor (pre-specified): _l = 307°, b = +9°_**

This is the centroid of the local anomaly cluster and was identified prior to any fanning analysis. No optimisation is involved. The fanning correlation from this direction (ρ = 0.986, exact p = 0.0028 on the 6-point core; ρ = 0.955, exact p = 0.0020 with the contested GRB point) constitutes the statistical basis for these predictions.

Robustness of the reference choice: a full-sky grid scan over reference directions finds no direction with a higher fanning correlation than the value already achieved from the Great Attractor (scan maximum ρ = 0.986 at l = 324°, b = −9°, 24° from the Great Attractor; look-elsewhere-corrected Monte Carlo p = 0.27). The pre-specified direction is therefore used alone, and no optimised secondary reference is registered.

## **Hypothesis**

The fanning pattern either continues beyond z ~ 2 (supporting a logarithmic or linear model and implying a measurable pre-CMB trunk) or saturates by z ~ 1–3 (supporting the sigmoid model and rendering the trunk geometrically invisible from the fanning pattern alone).

The evidential basis for this hypothesis is a significant correlation (exact p = 0.0028) built on a small verified sample (n = 6). The three models fitted with 2–3 free parameters each are weakly distinguished by the current data: the sigmoid is preferred by AIC on the core dataset, and the preference vanishes when the contested GRB point is included. The predictions below are registered as a high-risk, exploratory test designed to obtain the data needed to confirm, refine, or refute the fanning hypothesis.

## **Measurement requirements**

- **Observable:** Any large-scale directional signal, including but not limited to a 21cm power-spectrum dipole, quasar-density dipole, Lyman-α flux dipole, galaxy-count asymmetry, or baryon acoustic oscillation anisotropy.

- **Redshift:** z = 6–10 is the primary target range; z = 3–4 is a secondary target range and would partially constrain the sigmoid–logarithmic boundary.

- **Coordinates:** Reported in Galactic (l, b) with 1σ directional uncertainty.

- **Precision:** A directional uncertainty of σ_θ < 20° is sufficient to decide the primary question, saturation versus continuation, because the sigmoid and the continued-fanning family differ by roughly 38° at z = 8. Separating the logarithmic from the linear model requires σ_θ ≲ 6°, since those curves differ by roughly 12° at z = 8; measurements with 6° < σ_θ < 20° will be treated as deciding the primary question only.

- **Blindness:** The analysis should remain blind to these predictions until the directional result has been finalised.

## **Analysis plan**

**1.** Extract the dipole direction from the target dataset using standard spherical harmonic decomposition (ℓ = 1 mode) or an equivalent method.

**2.** Convert the result to Galactic coordinates (l, b).

**3.** Compute the deviation angle: _θ_GA = arccos(n̂_obs · n̂_ref), where n̂_ref = (l = 307°, b = +9°)._

**4.** Compare the measured θ with the predictions above.

**5.** Select the best-fitting model by minimising _|θ_obs − θ_pred| / σ_θ_ across models.

**6.** If a continued-fanning model is favoured, update the fanning-curve fit with the new data point and recompute the trunk extrapolation with full parameter-covariance error propagation.

## **Exclusion criteria**

Measurements with σ_θ > 30° will be treated as inconclusive because they are not precise enough to separate the model families.

Results dominated by foreground systematics, for example an ecliptic-aligned dipole caused by satellite scanning strategy or contamination from the Galactic plane, will be excluded from model comparison.

## **Falsification criteria**

**Model-level:** Each model is disfavoured if the measured θ falls outside its predicted range at > 2σ significance.

**Hypothesis-level:** If any single measurement at _z = 6–10 with σ_θ < 20°_ falls outside the predicted range of all three models by more than 2σ, the fanning hypothesis is falsified. This is the primary kill condition.

**Framework-level:** If three or more independent measurements at z = 3–50 each deviate more than 40° from all model predictions, the fanning pattern is not a feature of the data and the trunk extrapolation methodology is invalidated. (A reviewer suggested tightening this threshold to 30°; we retain 40° because measurement uncertainties of σ_θ ~ 20–30° mean a tighter threshold could be triggered by noise. The hypothesis-level single-measurement kill provides the sharper constraint for high-precision data.)

## **Context**

This pre-registration accompanies a philosophical framework (_"Nothing, Described: A Structural Ontology of Everything"_) and its cosmological companion paper. Together, they predict that the observable universe should exhibit a genealogical branching structure that may be detectable as directional fanning of cosmological anomalies with redshift.

Prior testing of the framework's genealogical predictions is reported in the companion paper. On the verified core dataset, that work includes a confirmed directional clustering of the 6 independent anomalies (mean pairwise separation 62.4° against a random expectation of 90°, Monte Carlo p = 0.019) and an equivalent result from a model-independent kinship metric combining angular separation and redshift depth (p = 0.017). The four-point additive-tree condition on the kinship metric, the framework's unique geometric signature, passes at significance on the verified catalogue (11 of 15 quartets on the core against a random expectation near 5, p ≈ 0.02; 29 of 35 with the contested point, p ≈ 0.001; robust across matching tolerances), with the caveat that the statistic is correlated with the directional clustering rather than independent of it. Explicit null results accompany these findings: an apparent physics-type pairing effect fully explained by geometric competition, a cross-redshift void prediction that was not unique against a 39% random baseline, and an optimised-reference correlation that does not survive look-elsewhere correction.

If a continued-fanning model is confirmed, the trunk extrapolation would constitute a geometric estimate of pre-CMB structural depth derived from the multi-redshift evolution of cosmic anisotropy, independent of the CMB power spectrum. Its current uncertainty band (15–69 e-folds under the logarithmic model) overlaps the Planck 2018 inflationary range (N = 50–60) without independently constraining it; future data would either sharpen or eliminate this estimate.
